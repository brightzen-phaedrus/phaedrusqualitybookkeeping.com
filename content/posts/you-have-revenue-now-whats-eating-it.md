---

## The Engine You Already Have

Every business runs the same engine. Revenue minus COGS minus Sales & Marketing equals Contribution. Contribution minus Overhead equals Profit. Two kinds of people work in every business: people **in the engine** (serving customers, acquiring and retaining them) and people **on the engine** (the pit crew — finance, ops, HR, IT).

When you have revenue, you have data. Real data. Not assumptions about what customers might do — actual records of what they did. How much they spent. What it cost you to serve them. Whether they came back.

That data tells a story most businesses aren't reading.

---

## What the Data Actually Says

Here's what we see when we look at businesses with $2M–$50M in revenue:

**COGS is higher than it needs to be.** Not because the product is expensive to make — because the process of making it, delivering it, or supporting it has accumulated manual steps that nobody has questioned in years. Somebody is copying data between systems. Somebody is reconciling spreadsheets. Somebody is doing a task that was necessary when you had 50 customers and is now insane at 5,000.

**Acquisition costs are climbing.** The channels that got you here are saturating. Your first S-curve is flattening, and nobody's mapped the next one. Marketing is spending more to maintain, not to grow.

**Retention is a black box.** You know your revenue. You might know your churn rate. But you probably can't tell me which customers are profitable and which ones are costing you money. You can't segment by lifetime gross profit because nobody's connected the operational data to the financial data.

**Overhead is growing faster than revenue.** Every new hire on the pit crew — every ops person, every analyst, every coordinator — is there because a process got too complex for the people already doing it. The headcount isn't solving the problem. It's managing the symptoms.

## Where AI Actually Matters

Most companies thinking about AI are asking the wrong question. They're asking: *What can AI do?* The right question is: *Where is the work that shouldn't exist?*

At Assembled Systems, we start with the engine — the same Contribution Engine framework. But instead of building it from assumptions, we instrument it from your actual data. We connect your operational systems to your financial systems and map where value is being created and where it's being destroyed.

Then we look for three things:

**1. Repetitive manual work.** Tasks that a human is doing the same way, hundreds of times, that follow a pattern an AI system can learn. Data entry. Invoice processing. Order routing. Customer classification. These aren't "AI opportunities" — they're operational debt that's been accumulating for years.

**2. High-COGS processes.** Anywhere your cost of goods sold includes labor that isn't creating value for the customer. If a human is spending 40% of their time on a task that exists only because two systems don't talk to each other, that's not a staffing problem. That's a systems problem.

**3. Decision bottlenecks.** Places where work stops and waits for a human to look at something, make a judgment, and push it forward. Approvals, exceptions, quality checks, triage. Many of these decisions follow patterns that are learnable — and the delay they introduce is invisible on every report except the one that measures how long your customer waited.

## The Twelve Levers, Revisited

In the [Startup Business Engineering article](/posts/startup-business-engineering-work-session/), we introduced the twelve levers that control every business. Pre-revenue, those levers are theoretical. Post-revenue, they're measurable — and AI can pull several of them simultaneously.

**Reduce cost of goods.** Automate the manual steps in fulfillment, support, and delivery. Every dollar you take out of COGS drops straight to contribution.

**Lower customer acquisition cost.** AI-powered segmentation and targeting means spending less to reach the right customers. Not more spend — smarter spend.

**Drive repeat purchases.** When you can actually see which customers are profitable and what drives retention, you can build systems that act on that knowledge automatically. Not a quarterly retention campaign — a continuous system that knows when a customer is drifting and what to do about it.

**Improve CAC payback time.** Speed up the time from first dollar spent to first dollar of contribution. That's a cash flow lever, and in a business burning working capital, it's the difference between growing and dying.

**Segment customers by profitability.** This one is almost always manual, almost always done annually, and almost always too late to be useful. An AI system that segments in real time changes what your team sees every morning when they sit down.

## The Weekly Feedback Loop

The Integrated Financial Model gets managed weekly. Fifty-two times a year, you compare what actually happened to what you said would happen.

When AI systems are doing the repetitive work, that loop gets tighter. Data flows in faster. Anomalies surface sooner. The model reflects reality in near real-time instead of waiting for someone to update a spreadsheet.

If you aren't managing your business weekly, you're managing your business weakly. And if the systems that feed your weekly cadence are manual, every cycle takes longer than it should.
