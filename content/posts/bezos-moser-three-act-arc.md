Two founders, twenty-five years apart, running the same three-act play: sell a commodity, build the infrastructure to survive it, then sell the infrastructure to everyone else.

<div style="margin-top: 32px;">
<span style="display: inline-block; background: #B7E1CD; color: #000; font-weight: 700; font-size: 0.75em; letter-spacing: 1.5px; text-transform: uppercase; padding: 4px 14px; border-radius: 999px;">Act 1</span>
</div>

## Sell the Commodity to Learn the Problem

<div style="border: 1px solid rgba(128,128,128,0.4); border-radius: 10px; padding: 12px 16px; margin-bottom: 10px;"><b>Bezos:</b> Sold books online <span style="color: #737373;">→</span> <b>Amazon</b></div>
<div style="border: 1px solid rgba(128,128,128,0.4); border-radius: 10px; padding: 12px 16px; margin-bottom: 14px;"><b>Moser:</b> Shipped books <span style="color: #737373;">→</span> <b>ShipCalm</b></div>

The commodity phase looks like the business. It isn't. It's the classroom where the real problems introduce themselves — at full volume, with real money on the line.

<div style="margin-top: 32px;">
<span style="display: inline-block; background: #F4C7C3; color: #000; font-weight: 700; font-size: 0.75em; letter-spacing: 1.5px; text-transform: uppercase; padding: 4px 14px; border-radius: 999px;">Act 2</span>
</div>

## Build Infrastructure for Yourself

<div style="border: 1px solid rgba(128,128,128,0.4); border-radius: 10px; padding: 12px 16px; margin-bottom: 10px;"><b>Bezos:</b> Hosting infrastructure to run Amazon <span style="color: #737373;">→</span> <b>AWS</b></div>
<div style="border: 1px solid rgba(128,128,128,0.4); border-radius: 10px; padding: 12px 16px; margin-bottom: 14px;"><b>Moser:</b> AI infrastructure to run ShipCalm <span style="color: #737373;">→</span> <b>Marvin</b></div>

Nobody sets out to build the infrastructure business. The commodity business forces it into existence. You build it because you have to survive — which is exactly why it works. It was battle-tested before it was ever a product.

<div style="margin-top: 32px;">
<span style="display: inline-block; background: #B7E1CD; color: #000; font-weight: 700; font-size: 0.75em; letter-spacing: 1.5px; text-transform: uppercase; padding: 4px 14px; border-radius: 999px;">Act 3</span>
</div>

## Sell the Infrastructure to Everyone Else

<div style="border: 1px solid rgba(128,128,128,0.4); border-radius: 10px; padding: 12px 16px; margin-bottom: 10px;"><b>Bezos:</b> AWS to the professional service industry <span style="color: #737373;">→</span> <b>Enterprise</b></div>
<div style="border: 1px solid rgba(128,128,128,0.4); border-radius: 10px; padding: 12px 16px; margin-bottom: 14px;"><b>Moser:</b> PULSE+ to the professional service industry <span style="color: #737373;">→</span> <b>SMB</b></div>

The infrastructure that ran your own company becomes the product. Bezos took it upmarket to the enterprise. Moser takes it to the SMB — the market the enterprise players can't profitably reach.

<div style="background: #000; color: #fff; border-radius: 12px; padding: 24px 28px; margin-top: 36px;">
  <div style="color: #B7E1CD; font-weight: 700; font-size: 0.75em; letter-spacing: 2px; text-transform: uppercase; margin-bottom: 8px;">The Insight</div>
  <div style="font-size: 1.25em; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.35; margin-bottom: 10px;">The commodity business was never the business. <span style="color: #B7E1CD;">It was the tuition.</span></div>
  <div style="color: #D4D4D4; line-height: 1.6;">Bezos paid it with books. Moser paid it with boxes. The real product is the infrastructure you were forced to build to survive the commodity phase — and the second act only works because the first act made the problems real.</div>
</div>
