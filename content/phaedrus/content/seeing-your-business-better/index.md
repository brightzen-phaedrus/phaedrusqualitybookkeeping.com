## Page 1 — Driving with a Dirty Windshield

The problem story. Short, visceral, no jargon yet.

1. **Open with the scene** (3–4): Running a business can feel like driving with a dirty windshield — while your accountant is carefully cleaning the back window. Keep both images.
2. **The app sprawl** (5): You run the business off QuickBooks, Shopify, Facebook Ads, Google Analytics, HubSpot, Stripe, Chase, spreadsheets — each app a keyhole view.
3. **The matrix** (6–7): Staring at all that data is like trying to see through the matrix. The result: **you make decisions with bad information.**
4. **What that costs** (8–10): Growth flattens and you can't say why (8); cash dwindles (9); it feels like spraying cash out the muffler to make the car go faster (10). Figures: the flat-revenue chart and cash-burn chart.
5. **Turn** (11): There is a better way.

Pull quote: "You make decisions with bad information."
Figures: windshield photo, app-logo collage, flat-growth chart, cash chart, muffler image.
Bridge: "Before I show you the better way, I should explain why an engineer — not an accountant — found it."

## Page 3 — The Weekly Accounting System

The system chapter. This is the conceptual heart; keep it concrete with real screenshots.

1. **Step one: get all the data** (22): Connect everything — QuickBooks, Shopify, ads, bank, CRM — and build an **Integrated Financial Model** (IFM). Figure: IFM screenshot.
2. **The new rowset** (25, 42–43): The **Statement of Economic Quality** — Audience → Customer Roll Forward → Unit Economics → (Income Statement, Balance Sheet below). Walk the SEQ story arc (36–40): every business has an audience → converts it into new customers → keeps them coming back → from that, unit economics, each period **and cumulatively**.
3. **One model, every zoom level** (26–30): Monthly, quarterly, annually — plus forecast (revenue growth and cash) and goals (performance metrics). Collapse these five build slides into one annotated figure.
4. **Run it weekly** (31–35): Monday Morning Metrics — manage weekly and average weekly. The quote slide (33): *"If you aren't managing your business weekly, you are managing your business weakly."* Example: DTC Q3 goals and weekly variance vs forecast (34–35).
5. **Why it works** (23, 44): It creates a **feedback loop** — unit economics and statements looking back, assumptions and forecast looking forward. And: "When you put the right data in front of an empowered team, they get better" (2x2 figure).
6. **Proof preview** (45): Home-care client chart — care hours/day climbing after implementation, negative to positive gross margins. Tease the full journey (Page 5).

Pull quote: weekly/weakly.
Bridge: "The engine inside the system is unit economics. Let's open the hood."

## Page 4 — Unit Economics: The Frequency Codes of Your Business

The teaching chapter — closest to a reference guide, still first person. Consolidate the components diagram (appears at 24, 41, 47, 54, 101…) to one canonical figure used once here.

1. **Why bother** (48–50): Know what it costs to acquire a customer, what each generates, what it costs to serve, profit per customer — so you know what to do next: spend more on marketing? do customers like the product? is the channel saturated? how much can I grow?
2. **The idea** (51–53): Analyze the business per unit. Unit economics are the "frequency codes" of your business — a crystal ball: better forecasts, visible growth limiters, focused attention.
3. **The components** (47): One diagram — Ad Spend + New Customers → **CAC**; Purchases/Customer × AOV × Gross Margin → **LTGP**; the headline ratio **LTGP:CAC**.
4. **CAC walkthrough** (55–59): Ad spend (~$100–200k/mo) ÷ new customers (~1,000–1,500/mo) = CAC ($100–175). Monthly points bounce; **the cumulative line is the signal in the noise** (59 — key chart).
5. **LTGP walkthrough** (60–68): The simple parts first — AOV ($200→$225 cumulatively) and gross margin (40–60% monthly, ~50% cumulative). Then purchases per customer: 85,601 orders ÷ 50,312 customers = **1.7x**, grown from 1.5x.
6. **Deep-dive sidebar** (69–71): Four real client PPC charts — "what insights can you draw?" Works as an interactive aside or collapsible section.

Pull quote: "The cumulatives are the signal in the noise."
Bridge: "Knowing the math is easy. Getting a company to live by it is the journey."

## Page 5 — Implementing Weekly Accounting

The change-management chapter. Honest about the awkward middle phase.

1. **It's a journey** (73–77): **Troubled → Functional → Integrated → World Class.** Maturity table (77) verbatim — data quality row ("monthly close by an accountant" → "the unit of work is self-aware") and feels-like row ("We won't know revenue until the books are closed" → "Where'd you get that data? That's not right!" → "We know where to focus our attention" → "Every role has an intention and a rowset").
2. **The three build steps** (78–83): 1) Get access to all your business data. 2) Build the weekly data warehouse — Instrumentation Pyramid (Forecast / Run the Business / Common Transactional Detail) creating one clean source of transactional truth. 3) Build the toolkit — Monday Morning Metrics to run the week, IFM to forecast monthly/quarterly/annually.
3. **The common rowset in practice** (84–88): SEQ from audience to cash; focus the team weekly on the drivers; track LTGP:CAC each period and cumulatively.
4. **Why weekly wins** (90–92): Feedback loops make systems stable (damped-oscillation figure — Functional oscillates hard, World Class hums). **52 feedback loops a year beat 12.** Calendar figure.
5. **Case study: the home-care company** (93–95): Functional phase = the team aligning on what the numbers mean (that's the "that's not right!" period — normalize it). Then productivity climbs, margins flip positive. Right data + empowered team → things get better.
6. **Landing** (89): "Once you see your business weekly, the old ways of seeing your business will seem quaint."

Pull quote: 52 > 12, or "quaint."
Bridge: "Here's what this looks like when the numbers turn against you — and the system catches it."

## Page 6 — Case Study: When Growth Is a Vanity Metric

The payoff chapter — the deck's best story. Tell it as a mystery with the reveal in the numbers.

1. **The envy setup** (98): DTC company: $20M annualized revenue, 6x growth, zero outside investors. Everyone wants to be them.
2. **The thesis** (99–100): **Revenue growth is a vanity metric. Unit economics are more important than the P&L.**
3. **The clue** (103–105): Ad spend scaling $30k → $500k/mo; new customers up — but CAC creeping $16 → $93.
4. **P&L by Customer** (107–115): LTGP sets the CAC target. Build the per-customer P&L once, as one table: 4.3 orders × $84 AOV = $361 lifetime revenue × 29% GM = **$106 LTGP**; CAC $33 → $73 contribution per customer, 20% contribution margin, **3.2x LTGP:CAC**. (Collapse the seven build slides.)
5. **The slide into trouble** (116–120): Quarter by quarter: 3.2x → 2.4x → 1.8x → **1.1x in October**. Meanwhile the standard statements show revenue up and net income shrinking — the P&L tells you something's wrong; unit economics tell you *what*.
6. **Call it what it is** (121–122): Running with unprofitable unit economics is not "investing in the business." It's spraying cash out the muffler. (Muffler image returns — callback to Page 1.)
7. **The fix** (123–130): Go back to when it was good. Set a profitable CAC: maintain CAC < $45, Facebook < $325k/quarter (< $25k/week), on-platform ROAS > 53x. Sidebar: CAC vs on-platform ROAS (accurate/tied-to-cash/slow vs real-time/directional) — give the ad agency direction in ROAS terms.
8. **The objection and the punchline** (131–132): "But if I have to reduce my ad spend, my business will shrink!" — **"Exactly!!"** Keep this beat intact; it's the talk's signature moment.
9. **The deeper law** (133–140): Every channel saturates. A business is not limited by the size of the market — it's limited by **the size of the channels through which it can profitably acquire customers.** Sigmoid figure: $300k already buys the ~1,600 customers the channel holds; the last $50k buys almost nothing. Spend less.
10. **What's next for this company** (142): Maximize contribution from the saturated channel; reduce ad spend and raise prices (LTGP:CAC > 2); find new channels to grow.
11. **Series close + CTA**: Loop back to the windshield — this is what a clean windshield shows you. The series' one service CTA: talk to us (contact/intake link), with the fullbook byline beneath.

Pull quotes: "Exactly!!" exchange; the size-of-channels law.
