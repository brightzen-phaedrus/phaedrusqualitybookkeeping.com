The unit-economics layer of the [Fourth Statement](/phaedrus/content/knowledge-garden/fourth-statement/): what does each customer actually contribute, and what does growth actually cost?

## The Core Questions

- **CAC** — what does it cost to acquire a customer?
- **LTGP** — what gross profit does that customer generate over their life?
- **The ratio** — is the machine profitable, and is it getting more or less efficient over time?

Simple questions. Most small businesses can't answer them — not because the math is hard, but because the data lives scattered across an ad platform, a storefront, and an accounting file that don't talk to each other. The [IFM](/phaedrus/content/knowledge-garden/ifm/) connects them.

## Contribution Thinking

Revenue is vanity; contribution is sanity. A sale that costs more to acquire and fulfill than it returns isn't growth — it's an expensive way to stay busy.

The Contribution Engine reframes the income statement around what each layer *contributes*: revenue, minus cost of goods, minus the marketing that produced it — before fixed costs. That number, tracked weekly in the [MMM](/phaedrus/content/knowledge-garden/mmm/) and per-unit in the [SEQ](/phaedrus/content/knowledge-garden/seq/), tells you whether the engine works.

## The Org Chart

A business is a system with two kinds of people:

**People working IN the Contribution Engine:**
- People who service customers directly
- People who attract and retain customers

**People working ON the Contribution Engine** (the pit crew):
- Instrument the engine
- Finance the engine
- Staff the engine
- Oversee the system as a whole

By organizing a business around the contribution engine, it becomes easier to make the unit of work self-aware and align the entire organization.

## Marginal vs Cumulative

The engine is watched two ways: **marginally** (what did the *last* customer cost and contribute?) and **cumulatively** (what has the whole cohort returned?). Healthy businesses can degrade at the margin long before the cumulative numbers show it — watching both is how you catch the turn early.
