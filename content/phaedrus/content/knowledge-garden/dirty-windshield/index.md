## Unit Economics

When words and math combine to predict things, we call that science. This system is the science of forecasting your business with unit economics.

Unit economics is a way of looking at your business one customer at a time. It answers the most important questions:

![Unit economics — a way of looking at your business one customer at a time](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/dw-one-customer-at-a-time.jpg)

1. How much does it cost to acquire one customer?
2. How many purchases per customer can I expect?
3. What is the gross profit over that customer's life?

Here's what that looked like for Matt.

### Customer Acquisition Cost

He spends about $100,000 per quarter on ads:

![Quarterly ad spend](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p08-0.png)

He gets between 200 and 400 new customers each quarter:

![New customers per quarter](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p09-0.png)

That's a little under $400 to acquire a customer. Quarterly results fluctuate, but the dark line — cumulative customer acquisition cost — is smooth. That's the [signal in the noise](/phaedrus/content/knowledge-garden/signal-vs-noise/). It's simple: total ad spend for all time divided by total new customers ever acquired.

![Customer Acquisition Cost — quarterly and cumulative](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p09-1.png)

### Average Order Value

Matt's AOV rose from $325 to $335, and more recently about $345 per customer per month:

![Average monthly revenue per customer](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p10-0.png)

### Gross Margin

Fluctuates just below 50%:

![Gross margin — quarterly and cumulative](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p10-1.png)

### Repeat Purchases

Many people do cohort analysis to understand customer life. Cohort analysis is complicated and hides the true picture. We simply look at the business's order table — the data table where they bill every customer.

From 31,000 purchases across 3,000 unique customers, we see 10.5 purchases per customer:

![31,000 purchases across 3,000 unique customers = 10.5 purchases per customer](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p11-0.png)

Lately, customer life has been about 15 months:

![Purchases per customer and customer life](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p11-1.png)

### Lifetime Gross Profit

That gives Matt about $2,500 in gross profit per customer lifetime:

![Purchases per customer × AOV × gross margin = lifetime gross profit](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p12-0.png)

Divide that by his CAC, and you get a 4–6x return on customer acquisition cost:

![Return on customer acquisition cost](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p12-1.png)

## Focus on Contribution, Not Revenue

Contribution is gross profit after sales and marketing. Contribution margin is contribution divided by net revenue.

![Contribution is your profit after sales and marketing expenses](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/dw-contribution-after-sm.jpg)

![Unit economics down to contribution margin](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p12-2.png)

Lifetime gross profit to CAC closely resembles contribution margin:

![Contribution margin vs LTGP:CAC](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p13-0.png)

The [Contribution Engine](/phaedrus/content/knowledge-garden/contribution-engine/) spans the business from the widest part of the sales and marketing funnel, through payments from new and repeat customers, to delivering on the promise. This is the engine that generates profit for the company. Everything else is overhead. To be profitable, contribution must be greater than overhead.

![Contribution must be greater than overhead](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/dw-contribution-overhead.jpg)

![Optimize for contribution, not revenue](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/dw-optimize-contribution.jpg)

![Contribution vs operating expense](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p13-1.png)

## Seek Growth Limits Courageously

Unit economics also answers another very important question: **how much can I grow?**

![How big can I grow the business?](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/dw-how-big-can-i-grow.jpg)

Sometimes cash is the limiter of growth. Sometimes capacity. Sometimes we get in our own way — and sometimes the market tells us the truth.

## Every Channel Saturates

It's a core tenet:

![A business is not limited by the size of the market — it's limited by the size of the channels through which it can profitably acquire customers](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p14-0.png)

Example: a business spent $300,000 to acquire 1,600 customers. Then $350,000 to acquire the same 1,600 customers:

![New customers vs ad spend — diminishing returns](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p15-0.png)

That's a saturated channel. Time to spend less there, and find new channels to grow:

![The saturated channel — spend less, find new channels](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/waw-p15-1.png)

Unit economics are a powerful map to understanding and forecasting a business.
