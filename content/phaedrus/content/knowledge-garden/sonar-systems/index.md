A sonar does three things:

1. **Samples data** from its environment
2. **Separates the signal from the noise**
3. **Creates a map** of the territory

![Sonars do what people do](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/slide-sonar-does.jpg)

People do the same thing:

1. Gather data from their senses and stories they are told
2. Separate what they value from what they don't
3. Create a mental map from what they value — to determine what to pursue and how to behave

![People in business do too](/phaedrus/knowledge-garden/images/slide-people-do.jpg)

This framing comes from a walk around the Chithurst Monastery in England. Surrounded by rigid routines, practices, and silence, the question appeared: how do people know what to believe? How do they separate the [signal from the noise](/phaedrus/content/knowledge-garden/signal-vs-noise/)?

## Applied to Finance

Most financial reporting is noise. Not wrong — noise. The numbers are accurate and the picture is still unreadable. Conventions designed for compliance bury the things an owner actually needs to see.

The [Fourth Statement](/phaedrus/content/knowledge-garden/fourth-statement/) exists because of this problem. It's a better sonar system for a business: sample the right data (from all business systems, not just the bank), separate the signal (unit economics, customer health, cash movement) from the noise (accrual artifacts, formatting conventions), and create a map (the [IFM](/phaedrus/content/knowledge-garden/ifm/)) that reflects the territory.

See the poem [Signal and Noise](/phaedrus/content/knowledge-garden/poems/signal-and-noise/).
