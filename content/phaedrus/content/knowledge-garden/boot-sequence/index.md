When you turn on a device, a small set of code loads from storage into memory. That code tells the device who it is supposed to be. Humans have a boot sequence too. We just don't choose it.

Most mornings, the first thoughts that fire are the same ones from yesterday: anxieties, regrets, a low hum of dread about the day ahead. That's your boot sequence running. You didn't write it. Parents, teachers, culture, and habit installed it years ago. It runs on autopilot.

The insight: you can rewrite the code.

## Installed Boot Sequences

We've been doing this to each other for thousands of years. The Scout Oath, the Pledge of Allegiance, religious creeds — all installed boot sequences. Statements of values repeated daily until they shape how a community sees the world.

Common ingredients: gratitude, respect, acceptance, forgiveness, awareness, confidence.

Ritual and repetition bind people together. That's not a bug. It's the mechanism by which culture transmits itself across generations.

## Writing Your Own

It is empowering to realize that we can intentionally install new assumptions about the world in our heads. These repeated intentions go deep into the subconscious and affect how we see what is happening around us.

This is not a way of thinking that is normally taught.

The Boot Sequence starts with a daily recitation — saying it out loud each morning:

> Today is the best day ever.
> I am free to do whatever I want.
> I am the master of my universe and
> I am the narrator of my own epic story.

Then you add lines specific to your own big vision. Your own art.

See the poem [Today is the Best Day Ever](/phaedrus/content/knowledge-garden/poems/today-is-the-best-day-ever/).

## Boot Sequences as Big Visions

Organizations have boot sequences too. We call them mission statements, but the good ones work the same way — repeated until the team believes them:

- "To connect everyone to an online world that improves the human condition."
- "A global community of individuals empowered to pursue the work they love."
- "To accelerate humanity's transition to sustainable energy."
- "To help everyone see their business better."

That last one is ours. The [Fourth Statement](/phaedrus/content/knowledge-garden/fourth-statement/), the [IFM](/phaedrus/content/knowledge-garden/ifm/), the weekly rhythm — it's a system built from a boot sequence.

Everything you've ever accomplished started as a story in your head. The question is whether you wrote the story or someone else did.
